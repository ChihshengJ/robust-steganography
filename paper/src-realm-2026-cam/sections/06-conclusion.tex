We have introduced a symmetric key method for purely \emph{semantic} text steganography, where stegotext encoding and decoding rely only on semantic features associated with the task input and on publicly accessible resources (e.g., an LLM API). To our knowledge, this is the first such approach that is robust to \emph{global} paraphrase-based attacks. We instantiate our method for three NLP tasks, empirically demonstrating the robustness of the resulting schemes under a range of paraphrase-based attacks---showing strong bitwise and perfect message recovery rates, even for relatively long messages, and even absent any error-correcting codes. Furthermore, we showed that perplexity- and embedding-based classifiers struggle to reliably discriminate covertexts and stegotexts, further testifying to the viability of our method.
